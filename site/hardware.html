1.2 · WIRE

Wire the controller

The ESP32-C3 stays on all the time. It reads the button, signals the Pi, and switches the PowerBoost's 5V output through a 2N2222.

Why open-drain?

GPIO 3 never drives voltage into a powered-off Pi. The Pi's pull-up holds the line high, and the ESP32 only pulls it low.

Power switching

The transistor "presses" the PowerBoost K pin. One tap turns 5V on, a double tap turns it off.

Pi-side wiring

INMP441 mic to the Pi's I2S pins, recording LED to BCM GPIO27. [ADD MIC PIN MAP]

1.3 · FLASH

Flash the ESP32

  1. Open controller/esp32_controller/esp32_controller.ino in the Arduino IDE. The folder name must match the sketch.
  2. Board: ESP32C3 Dev Module (esp32 core by Espressif).
  3. Turn on USB CDC On Boot for serial output.
  4. Upload, then open the Serial Monitor at 115200 baud.

Tune the feel: debounce, click window, long-press threshold and pulse lengths are constants at the top of the sketch.

Check: try every gesture

With the Serial Monitor open, each gesture should print a log line. The lower trace shows the signal the controller sends.

Single click

Toggles recording on the Pi.

Triple click

Powers the Pi on.

Long press

Fires on release. Shuts the Pi down cleanly, then cuts power.
